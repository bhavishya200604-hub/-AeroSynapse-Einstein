PINN training on real data inverse problem

Train a physics-informed neural network on measured soil temperatures: the network represents T(z, t), the thermal diffusivity α is learned with it, and the heat equation is enforced at collocation points. One sensor can be held out: the verified classical estimator is fitted on the same sensors and both predict it.

Network and optimiser

Scaled heat equation T̂τ = κ T̂ζζ, κ = α tw/L², residual divided by the daily frequency; float64; a data-only warm-up, then Adam and L-BFGS. The recorded study (5 seeds, 3 stations) is in docs/tables/phase10_5_real_pinn.md.

How to use the lab

  1. Simulator: pick a case, move a slider (or switch to physical units and type °C, m, W m⁻² K⁻¹). The verified solver and, where trained, the PINN ensemble answer; badges tell you when you leave the PINN's training range.
  2. Verify: grid convergence proves the solver; the parameter sweep shows where the PINN can be trusted.
  3. Inverse lab: switch the heat source off and see an identifiability failure before any fitting.
  4. Real data: fit soil diffusivity to NOAA measurements or upload your own table (time column + temperatures at known positions). Compare a homogeneous and a layered model on a hold-out window.
